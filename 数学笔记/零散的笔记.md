\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F &= (A+\bar{B})(\bar{C}+\bar{B})(A+\bar{C})  \\
&= (A+\bar{B})(A+\bar{C})(\bar{C}+\bar{B}) \\
&= (AA + A\bar{B} + A\bar{C} + \bar{B}\bar{C})(\bar{C}+\bar{B}) \\
&= (A + \bar{B}\bar{C})(\bar{C}+\bar{B}) \\
&= A\bar{C} + \bar{B}\bar{C}\bar{C} + A\bar{B} + \bar{B}\bar{B}\bar{C} \\
&= A\bar{C} + A\bar{B} + \bar{B}\bar{C}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

2. [Finite impulse response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_impulse_response#:~:text=In%20signal%20processing%2C%20a%20finite,to%20zero%20in%20finite%20time.)
	- In signal processing, a **finite impulse response (FIR) filter** is a filter whose impulse response (or response to any finite length input) is of finite duration, because it settles to zero in finite time.
	- In contrast to *infinite impulse response filter* which may have internal feedback and may continue to respond indefinitely (usually decaying).
